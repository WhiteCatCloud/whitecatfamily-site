Using the app

Just unboxed it? Start with the setup guide. Everything below assumes your router is already paired.

WhiteCat controls your home Wi-Fi. Anything connected to it — tablets, TVs, laptops, consoles, phones — follows your rules. A phone that leaves the house and switches to mobile data is no longer on your network, so home rules don't reach it. That's why the examples below use tablets, TVs and laptops: devices that live on your Wi-Fi.

Family members & devices

Open Family Members and tap Add Member. Give them a name and a Profile image so everyone is easy to spot at a glance.

A member is a person, not a device. You link their devices to them in the next step — that's what lets one rule cover someone's tablet, laptop and console at once.

Go to Devices → Wi-Fi Networks and tap your network. The app shows a QR code — point the new device's camera at it and it joins without anyone typing the password.

Useful for a child's tablet or a guest's laptop when you'd rather not read a long password out loud.

Anything that joins your Wi-Fi shows up under Devices. WhiteCat guesses the name and type from how the device identifies itself, so most arrive already labelled — "Samsung TV", "iPad".

For the ones that don't, the app has a purpose-built trick. Go to Devices → Wi-Fi Networks → Name a device. It shows a short-lived QR code — scan it with the device you're trying to identify, or open http://whitecat.lan in that device's browser. The router works out which device is asking and lets you name it on the spot.

The device has to already be on your Wi-Fi, and the code expires after about a minute — generate a new one if it lapses.

Rules & schedules

Family rules apply to everyone — the household baseline. Blocking adult content or gambling belongs here.

Member rules apply to one person and win where they disagree. So you can block TikTok for the whole family, then allow it for a teenager, without touching anyone else.

Open Schedules and tap Create Schedule. Give it a name you'll recognise later — "School nights", "Homework" — pick the days and the time window, then choose what it blocks.

Last step matters: add people under Assigned members. A schedule with nobody assigned does nothing, and the app will tell you so.

Swipe the schedule row to the right and tap +15m. It pushes the current block back by fifteen minutes for that person, then everything returns to normal on its own.

Common setups

The things most families ask for first.

Block YouTube on my son's tablet

  1. Make sure the tablet is assigned to him: Devices → the tablet → Family member.
  2. Open his profile → Member rules.
  3. Find YouTube in the service list and block it.

This covers every device linked to him, not just the tablet. To block it on the tablet alone, give the tablet its own member.

Bedtime for the whole family

  1. Schedules → Create Schedule, name it "Night".
  2. Sunday to Thursday, 21:00 to 07:00.
  3. Block streaming, gaming and social.
  4. Under Assigned members, add the children — or everyone, if the adults want the same discipline.

Leave the schedule running across midnight; it handles the rollover.

Stop the TV after 21:00, but leave the laptops alone

  1. Create a member called "Living room" and assign the TV to it.
  2. Create a schedule for 21:00 to 07:00 blocking streaming.
  3. Assign it to "Living room" only.

Members don't have to be people. A room or a single device works just as well when you want a rule to stay narrow.

No gaming sites on the laptop during homework

  1. Create Schedule, name it "Homework".
  2. Monday to Friday, 16:00 to 18:00.
  3. Block gaming — and social, if that's the real distraction.
  4. Assign it to the child.

Keep the window short. A rule that bites all evening gets argued with; one that ends at six doesn't.

"Five more minutes!"

  1. Open that person in the app.
  2. Tap +15m.

It expires on its own — no need to remember to put the rule back. Tap again if you're feeling generous.

Privacy & Data

The router does exchange the minimum needed for the service to run at all: whether it is online, its firmware version, and the rules you have set — that is what lets you change a rule from your phone while you are out. Where that data lives and how long it is kept is set out in the privacy policy.
