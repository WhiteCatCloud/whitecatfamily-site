Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 2026  ·  We don't sell your data. Ever.

The short version

WhiteCat is a tool for parents, built by parents. The thing most people worry about — a record of every site your family looks up — is switched off when the router leaves the factory. You turn it on yourself, in the app, and it fails closed: unless the router can positively read "on", it records nothing.

What the router sends us by default is duller: which devices are on your network, what those devices call themselves, and whether the router is healthy. It goes every 30 to 60 seconds, which means that kept together over a day it does show when each device was at home. Below we list all of it, name every company that receives any of it, and say how long we keep it.

We don't sell data to advertisers, and we never see the content of what your family reads, types or watches. Full stop.

Children's privacy

WhiteCat is operated by parents — accounts are created and managed by adults only. We do not knowingly collect personal information directly from children under 13. A family member profile in the app (for example "Emma, age 8") is created by you and holds only a name and an icon.

One thing worth knowing: a phone usually announces its own name on the network, and people name phones after their owner. So a child's first name commonly reaches us as part of a device name, and is included in the device-naming step described above. You can rename or delete any device in the app. If you believe we hold data from a child that we should not, email privacy@whitecatcloud.com and we will delete it.

What we never do

  • We never sell your data — not to advertisers, not to anyone
  • We never read the content of your family's internet traffic
  • We never build advertising profiles of you or your children
  • We never share your data with schools, governments or other companies beyond the ones named above, except where the law requires it
  • We never switch on the domain log or the screen time overview for you — that is your decision

Where your data is stored

The service runs on servers in the United States. Backups sit in Cloudflare's object storage, which is configured to choose its own region — so we cannot promise you that a backup stays inside the EU, and we are not going to pretend otherwise.

What does not leave your home is your traffic itself: the router does not proxy, mirror or store the pages, messages or video your family loads. What leaves it continuously is the device and health data listed above — roughly 36 MB a month for a household with nothing switched on, and around 140 MB with activity logging enabled.

Support access to your router

Every WhiteCat router keeps an outbound maintenance connection open to our servers, which lets our engineers log in to the device to diagnose faults. It is enabled on every router we ship, there is currently no switch to turn it off, and the app does not tell you when it is used. We are stating it plainly because it is true, and we regard the lack of a switch and the lack of an indicator as gaps in your control rather than features.

Cookies and this website

Separately from the router: this website uses Google Analytics to count visits. No analytics cookie is set until you accept it in the banner, and you can withdraw that at any time from the same banner. If you write to us using the form below, your name, email address and message reach us by email and are kept only as long as we need them to answer you. None of this has anything to do with your router or your family's devices, and we do not use it for advertising.

Your rights

You can ask us for a copy of your data, ask us to correct it, ask us to delete your account and everything attached to it, or object to a particular use. Email privacy@whitecatcloud.com. There is no self-service export button in the app yet, so we handle these by hand: we acknowledge within 5 business days and complete the request within 30 days. You can also switch the domain log and the screen time overview off in the app at any time. They take effect immediately and stay off until you turn them back on.

  • Art. 6(1)(a) — consent: the per-device DNS query log, the screen time overview, the anonymous data you choose to share, and analytics cookies on this website. All are off by default and you can withdraw consent at any time, with immediate effect.
  • Art. 6(1)(f) — legitimate interest: network security, and crash and error diagnostics to keep the product working.

Your rights

Under GDPR you have the right to access, rectify, erase, port, restrict the processing of, and object to the processing of your personal data, and to withdraw any consent you have given. We handle these requests manually: we acknowledge within 5 business days and complete them within 30 days. You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority — in Slovakia: Úrad na ochranu osobných údajov. EU customers may additionally file with their own national authority.

No selling, no advertising profiles

We do not sell personal data. We do not use your family's data for advertising, and we do not build profiles of your children.

We do use one automated step, and we would rather name it than let you find it: when a new device appears on your network, its identifiers and the names it broadcasts are sent to a model operated by Anthropic PBC so it can propose a readable device name. It produces a label for a piece of hardware. It makes no decision about a person and has no legal or similarly significant effect within the meaning of Art. 22 GDPR. There is no self-service switch that turns this off for your account today — every newly discovered device goes through it. You may object under Art. 21 GDPR and we will handle that by hand, and you can always set your own name for a device in the app, which takes precedence over the generated one.
